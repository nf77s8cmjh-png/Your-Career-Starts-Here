Start where you are

Welcome. We're glad you're here.

Starting your career can feel overwhelming, especially when you're figuring things out for the first time. This resource hub was created to help college students build professional skills, discover opportunities, and feel more confident taking their next steps.

You do not need to have everything figured out today. Use the sections below as a starting point, return whenever you need them, and take one step at a time.

“You don't need to have everything figured out to get started. Your experiences are valuable, your goals are worth pursuing, and you deserve access to opportunities.”
01 · Show what you bring

Build a stronger resume.

Your resume is more than a list of jobs. It is a way to show employers your skills, experiences, and potential.

What to include

  • Contact information: Your name, professional email, phone number, and LinkedIn URL.
  • Education: School, major, expected graduation date, and relevant coursework.
  • Experience: Jobs, internships, campus roles, volunteering, and projects.
  • Skills: Relevant technical and interpersonal skills.

Make your experience count

  • Start bullet points with action verbs such as organized, analyzed, supported, created, or managed.
  • Describe what you did and, when possible, include the result.
  • Use numbers to show scale or impact when you can do so accurately.
  • Customize your resume for each role and proofread before submitting.
Remember: You do not need an internship to have a strong resume. Part-time jobs, class projects, student organizations, and volunteer work can all demonstrate valuable skills.

Quick resume checklist

  • Is the layout easy to scan?
  • Are dates, titles, and formatting consistent?
  • Do bullet points explain your contributions?
  • Have you checked spelling, grammar, and contact information?
02 · Prepare with confidence

Get ready for interviews.

An interview is a chance to connect your experiences to the role and show an employer how you think, communicate, and solve problems.

Questions to practice

  1. Tell me about yourself.
  2. Why are you interested in this position?
  3. What is one strength you would bring to this role?
  4. Tell me about a challenge you faced and how you handled it.
  5. Why do you want to work for this organization?

Use the STAR method

  • Situation: Set the scene. What was happening?
  • Task: What were you responsible for?
  • Action: What steps did you personally take?
  • Result: What happened? What did you learn?
Before the interview: Research the organization, review the job description, practice your examples out loud, plan what you will wear, and prepare two or three thoughtful questions for the interviewer.

Questions you can ask: “What does success look like in this role?” · “How would you describe the team culture?” · “What are the next steps in the hiring process?”

03 · Build real connections

Start networking.

Networking is not about knowing everyone. It is about building genuine professional relationships and being open to learning from others.

Ways to get started

  • Attend campus career fairs, employer events, and alumni panels.
  • Join student organizations connected to your interests.
  • Connect with alumni and professionals on LinkedIn.
  • Ask professors, advisors, and mentors thoughtful questions.
  • Follow up after conversations and thank people for their time.

Sample LinkedIn message

Hi [Name], I'm a student at the University of Illinois studying [Major], and I'm exploring opportunities in [Industry]. I came across your profile and would love to connect and learn more about your career path. Thank you for considering!

Personalize the message before sending. Keep it brief, polite, and specific.

Try an informational interview: Ask a professional for 15–20 minutes to hear about their career path. Prepare a few questions, respect their time, and send a thank-you note afterward.
04 · Take the next step

Find internships and jobs.

You do not have to wait until graduation to start exploring your options. Small, consistent steps can help you find opportunities that match your interests.

Where to look

  • University career platforms and career fairs.
  • Company career pages.
  • LinkedIn job listings.
  • Professional associations and student organizations.
  • Faculty, alumni, and career advisors.

Stay organized

  • Keep a list of roles, companies, deadlines, and application links.
  • Save a copy of each resume and cover letter you submit.
  • Track interviews and follow-up dates.
  • Apply consistently and tailor materials to each position.
  • Ask for feedback and keep improving.
Rejection is not a measure of your potential. Hiring decisions depend on many factors. Keep learning, keep applying, and use each experience to strengthen your next application.

The purpose behind the project

Why I created this resource.

I created Your Career Starts Here as part of my B401 Purpose Project at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. I wanted to help first-generation college students access career resources that can sometimes feel difficult to navigate.

My original plan was to collaborate with Prospanica, a campus Registered Independent Organization, to organize a career and resume workshop. Since coordinating a workshop was not possible alongside the organization's other commitments, I decided to move forward with a digital resource hub instead.

My goal is to share this resource with Prospanica for possible distribution to students. I hope it helps students feel more confident pursuing internships, building professional relationships, and preparing for their futures.

Your background is part of your story. Let it be your strength.

This is an independent student project. It is not an official University of Illinois or Prospanica website, and the organizations listed are not necessarily sponsors or endorsers.
